How to Book a Doctor’s Appointment

What You Need First

  • Name
  • Date of birth
  • Reason for visit
  • Insurance info (if you have it)
  • Preferred date/time

Phone Scripts

Standard Appointment

“Hi, I’m calling to schedule an appointment. My name is ___ and my date of birth is ___.”

If You’re Nervous or Shy

“Hi, I’ve never done this before and I’m a little nervous, but I need to make a doctor’s appointment.”

If You Don’t Know What Kind of Doctor You Need

“I’m not sure what type of appointment I need. I can describe my symptoms.”

If They Speak Quickly

“Could you slow down a bit? I want to make sure I understand.”

If You Don’t Understand a Question

“I’m sorry, could you explain what that means?”

If You Need to Call Back

“Can I call back with that information later today?”

If You’re Under 18

“I’m under 18. Does a parent or guardian need to be present for this appointment?”

If You Don’t Have Insurance

“I don’t have insurance right now. Are there self-pay or reduced-cost options?”

If English Is Not Your First Language

“Do you have an interpreter available for this visit?”

If They Say There Are No Appointments

“Are there any cancellations or waitlists I could be added to?”

If You Need an Urgent Appointment

“My symptoms are getting worse. Is there a sooner appointment available?”

Leaving a Voicemail

“Hi, my name is ___. I’m calling to schedule an appointment. My date of birth is ___. You can call me back at ___. Thank you.”

What They Might Ask You

  • Symptoms
  • Preferred doctor
  • Any medications you take
  • Your phone number
  • What to bring:
    • ID (school ID is okay)
    • Insurance card
    • Medication list
    • Notes about symptoms
Emergency Notice

This guide is for educational purposes only.
If you are experiencing a medical emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room immediately.

Accessibility & Language Access

Healthcare should be accessible to everyone. You have the right to:

  • Request an interpreter
  • Ask for written instructions
  • Ask providers to speak more slowly or clearly
  • Request accommodations for disabilities
