Immigrant & First-Generation Health Help

You Are Allowed Care

You do not need to be a citizen to receive medical care in the United States.

Scripts

  • “I don’t have a Social Security number.”
  • “Do you accept patients without insurance?”

Only ask these if you are fully comfortable.

Your Rights

  • You can request an interpreter
  • Emergency care cannot be denied
  • You can ask questions without judgment

Before / During / After a Visit

Before

  • Write down symptoms
  • Bring insurance or ID if available
  • Arrive early if possible

During

  • Ask questions
  • Ask for clarification
  • Request privacy if needed

After

  • Ask when results will come back
  • Ask who to contact with questions
  • Write down next steps

Language Barriers

  • Ask for an interpreter — it’s free at hospitals
  • Bring written notes of symptoms

Helping Your Parents Understand Healthcare

  • Explain instructions in simpler terms
  • Translate pharmacy labels
  • Help schedule appointments

Affordable Care Options

  • Community clinics
  • Federally qualified health centers
  • Sliding-scale services
Emergency Notice

This guide is for educational purposes only.
If you are experiencing a medical emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room immediately.

Accessibility & Language Access

Healthcare should be accessible to everyone. You have the right to:

  • Request an interpreter
  • Ask for written instructions
  • Ask providers to speak more slowly or clearly
  • Request accommodations for disabilities
